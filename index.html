Licensed Conveyancer vs Solicitor

The source page makes a clear NSW distinction. A property matter may be handled by a licensed conveyancer, regulated by NSW Fair Trading under the Conveyancers Licensing Act 2003, or by a solicitor. It also notes that a licensed conveyancer specialises in property transfers, while a solicitor can advise on related legal issues. For a straightforward purchase or sale, the practical comparison is experience with the exact transaction type. First home buyers may need someone who can explain each step to settlement. A family title transfer needs careful document handling. Commercial matters can involve leases, GST and broader due diligence. Off-the-plan matters raise contract timing and disclosure questions that should be checked well before completion. If you are choosing between providers, ask less about titles and more about recent work on the same type of file, who will review the contract, and when complex issues are escalated. This focus on experience helps you select a professional who is familiar with the specific legal and practical requirements of your transaction.

Special Risks: Auctions and Off-the-Plan

Not every property file follows the same timetable. The source page specifically highlights first home buyer, off-the-plan, auction and commercial matters, and each one changes what should happen before you commit. For auction purchases, the major issue is timing. The page states there is no cooling-off period at auction in NSW, so the contract needs review before you bid rather than after. For private treaty purchases, the same source says most residential contracts in NSW have a 5 business day cooling-off period from exchange of contracts, ending at 5pm on the fifth business day. It does not apply to property bought at auction, and the vendor may keep your deposit if you withdraw. Off-the-plan buyers should focus on the contract terms and disclosure rules that apply when buying before construction is complete. Sellers should check that the contract for sale and prescribed disclosure documents are prepared correctly before the property is marketed. Commercial buyers and sellers should test whether the adviser is also checking lease terms, GST treatment and settlement requirements, not only transfer paperwork. Understanding these differences helps you choose a provider who is prepared for the specific challenges of your transaction type.

That gives you a simple checklist for comparing services, because each stage affects timing and cost visibility. Contract review is where unusual clauses, disclosure gaps or settlement dates are identified early. Searches help confirm what is being transferred and whether further questions are needed.
